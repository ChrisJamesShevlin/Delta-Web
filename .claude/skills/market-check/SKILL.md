# Market check (the £45 challenge)

Read the section "The £45 play-money challenge" in CLAUDE.md first. Those rules decide everything below. Nothing here is a proven edge: say so, plainly, every time.

## 0. Review open positions FIRST, before looking for anything new
First run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage reconcile --execute` (it records in the journal any trade IG closed on its own, by stop or take-profit, since the last session; it changes the journal only and places nothing; if it reports a close it could not match, tell the user). Then run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage list` at the start of every check (it also logs a position check, so the best and worst excursion can be estimated later). Managing an open trade is risk management and comes before new ideas. For each position look at its P&L in R, its stop and take-profit, the thesis and invalidation recorded at entry, and what has changed since. Then act, unprompted, under the user's standing authorisation (2026-11-11). The user's point: you trade without emotion, so you are better placed to decide, and **cutting losers short is where most traders fail**.

You may, without asking:
- **Close a position in loss or in profit** with `python3 -m ig_swing.manage close --deal-id ... --reason-type <thesis_invalidated|new_information|risk_event|time|reversal|profit_taking|other> --reason "<why>" --execute` (with `IG_LIVE_ORDERS=yes`). **Cut a loser early, before its stop, when its thesis is broken**: the invalidation you recorded has happened (a level broken, the catalyst reversed, the data went the other way) or the facts have changed. Never hold a dead thesis because the stop is not hit yet, and never hope.
- **Tighten a stop** (`manage stop ... --level|--breakeven|--distance`) or run the mechanical rule every check: `manage trail --execute` (stop to breakeven once up 1R, to +1R once up 2R). **Set or move the take-profit** (`manage limit`).
- **Reverse**: close with `--reason-type reversal`, then record a NEW idea in the opposite direction with its own honest probability, stop and target, and place it with `ig_swing.place`. A reversal is a fresh assessment, never "to win it back". The daily limit of two new trades and the cooldown after two losing trades still apply, and a cut loser counts as a loss: if they block the new entry, say so and do not look for a way round.
- **Trailing stop as standard once a trade is up 1R.** The stop starts fixed, sized to the idea. When `manage list` shows a position at +1R or more and not yet trailing, run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage trailing --deal-id ...` (dry run) and then with `--execute`: it moves the stop to breakeven and asks IG to trail it at the current gap (so it can never widen), which keeps working between sessions. **This API call has not been tested on a live position yet**, so check the result with `manage list` (it shows "IG trailing N points"). If IG refuses it, the command prints the exact manual steps and numbers: tell the user those steps in plain words and ask them to set it in the platform, then carry on with the mechanical `manage trail --execute` ratchet (it leaves positions IG is trailing alone). The shadow book scores every idea both ways (a fixed stop and target against a trailing stop from +1R at 1R, 1.5R and 2R behind the best price) and the report shows the comparison, so keep checking that it really helps.
- **Let winners run.** The take-profit set at entry is only a first target and must not cap a trade that is still working. When a position is near its take-profit, the thesis is intact and the move is still going (price holding above or below the breakout, the catalyst still in force, no reversal in the headlines), move the take-profit further out with `manage limit` and make sure the stop is at least at breakeven (`manage trail --execute` does the mechanical part, and a stop only ever tightens). Take the profit instead (`--reason-type profit_taking`) when the move is stalling, the thesis has been played out, or a risk event is due. Record why each time, so `manage history` can show whether letting winners run paid.
- Before a high-impact event (CPI, jobs, FOMC) in a market where you hold a position that is not an event trade, tighten to breakeven or close.
- At the end of an idea's time horizon with neither stop nor target hit, reassess and close if the thesis is not working (`--reason-type time`).

Rules for early exits: tie each one to a stated reason, not to discomfort or the clock. They are discretionary and can hurt (cutting a trade that would have worked), so run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage history` now and then: it compares early exits with stop and target hits, which is how we learn whether cutting early helps. Run each changing command first without `--execute` if you are unsure; the dry run shows exactly what would change.

Never: widen a stop, remove a stop, add to a position, average down, or touch an account other than the challenge account. A stop can only tighten (the client refuses otherwise). Tell the user at once after any action, in plain words: what, why, the result. Between sessions only IG's server-side stop and take-profit protect a position, so be conservative about leaving a position unprotected from a risk event.

## 1. Get the data (read-only)
The universe is NOT limited to a list. The user's instruction: we can trade anything, so do not narrow it. The briefing scans a default list so nothing obvious is missed, and anything else on IG can be looked at and traded.
- **Always start with the full picture.** Run `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge brief`: the default scan list of 32 markets, grouped into stock indices (US 500, Nasdaq, Dow, Russell 2000, DAX, FTSE, CAC, Nikkei), US shares (Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Tesla, Netflix, Intel), metals (gold, silver), energy (WTI, Brent, natural gas) and currencies (ten pairs). For all of them it shows price, move and spread; for everything except the shares also volatility (ATR), the 20-day range and a stake hint; IG client sentiment for every market IG publishes it for; and it adds the next 48 hours of calendar events, **earnings due in the next 3 days for the US shares**, general headlines and **news by market** (headlines that mention each market itself). It is about 30 IG calls, so **run it once per session**, not again within about 30 minutes. If you see `exceeded-api-key-allowance`, stop, tell the user, and do not retry in a loop.
- **If the user names a market or a move** ("gold just dropped", "Uber is falling", "Meta results tonight"), ALSO run `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge quick --market "<anything>"`. The market can be a name from the briefing, an IG epic, or plain search words: it is resolved through IG, so any share, index, currency, commodity, bond or ETF that IG offers as a daily bet works. Run it for two or three related markets too. It adds the fit check (margin and minimum stake against the account), the move in daily ranges, the last 24 hours, IG client sentiment, the calendar, earnings for a US share in the next 7 days, and the headlines that mention that market.
- **To look beyond the list** use `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge find --term "<words>"`: it lists matching IG markets with spread, minimum stake, margin and whether the minimum stake fits the account. Find ideas outside the list yourself with WebSearch (biggest movers, earnings reports, news), then check them with `find` and `quick`. Shares and indices outside the briefing are fair game.
- **News and sentiment must cover everything you consider,** not only the markets the briefing printed: for any market you are weighing, make sure you have looked at its news (the briefing's news-by-market line, the quick read, and a WebSearch for the cause) and its IG client sentiment where IG publishes one. Say so when there is none.
- **You are free to recommend something else.** "No trade in the market you named" is a valid answer: say so plainly, and mention a better idea elsewhere if there is one. Scan every class, do not default to currencies. At £45 many large shares and indices cannot be held even at the minimum stake (margin); the briefing and `find` say which fit. Those still go in the shadow book. For repeat checks in the same session use quick reads on specific markets instead of re-running the full briefing.

What the data does and does not cover, so you do not overclaim: the calendar is US releases (BLS and FRED dates, the Fed's meeting dates) with times only where BLS gives them, and no forecasts or consensus numbers; there is no UK or euro-area calendar. Earnings dates come from Finnhub's free US calendar (EPS estimates where it has them) and only for US shares. Headlines are titles only from the free RSS feeds and Finnhub. IG client positioning comes from IG's own sentiment feed. If the cause of a move is not in this data, use WebSearch to look for it, and say plainly when you still cannot find one.

## 1b. Research pass (for any idea you might take live, and for any sudden move you cannot explain)
Use WebSearch and WebFetch for about three to six lookups, not an open-ended trawl. Aim to find:
- the **cause and timing** of the move, from a primary or wire source (a central bank statement, the BLS or other official release, Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, the company filing) rather than a blog;
- for a data release, the **forecast against the actual number**: the surprise is what moves price, and the free calendar has no forecasts;
- **how much is already priced in**: when the news broke and how far price has moved since, because a move that has already happened is not a trade;
- the other side: the best argument against the idea, and what would prove it wrong.
- IG's own public analyst page, `https://www.ig.com/uk/news-and-trade-ideas` (WebFetch, no login): read the newest items and any article about the market you are weighing (earnings previews, the levels IG's analysts watch). It is commentary published a few times a day, not a live wire, and it is not the platform's in-app news or push alerts, which sit behind the user's login and which the API does not give us. If the user pastes a headline from a notification, treat it as the freshest source and say whether it gives a direction you would back. Count it as a web source in `--research web --source <url>`.
Record how deep you went with `--research none|headlines|web` and the sources you relied on with `--source` (repeatable). More reading is not an edge: markets absorb news in seconds and you are reading it late, so do not raise `--p-win` because a story sounds convincing. A persuasive narrative found after the move is a reason for suspicion. The report compares results by research depth, which is how we find out whether reading more helps. Flag secondary or promotional sources as such.

## 1c. Technical read (added 2026-11-12)
The briefing prints a **Technical read** block for every market with daily bars, and `quick` prints one for the market you name: trend against the 20-day average and its five-day slope, RSI(14), distance in ATRs to the 20-day high and low (a minus sign means beyond it), and the prior day's and last five days' highs and lows. It is archived with every snapshot and saved with every idea. Use it to confirm or contradict a driver and to place stops and targets at real levels, not as a signal on its own (the technical rules alone showed no edge). A news idea that fights the trend, or a breakout into an extreme RSI, gets a lower honest `--p-win` or no trade. Since 2026-11-12 it also carries a **longer view from Yahoo** (free, no IG allowance used): distance from the 50 and 200 day averages and whether they are aligned, and the US shares get a full read too. Yahoo is a different instrument from IG's daily bet (cash index, front future, spot currency), so its levels are rescaled and approximate; IG's own bars still set ATR and stops. If Yahoo fails the briefing says so and carries on.

## 1d. Macro backdrop (added 2026-11-12)
The briefing prints a **Macro backdrop** block: the US 10-year yield, the dollar index and the VIX, with the move today and over five sessions (Yahoo, same day, no key). It is archived as a `macro_backdrop` event every briefing. Use it as context: a rising dollar and yield usually press on gold and on currencies against the dollar, and a jumping VIX says risk appetite is falling. No rule turns it into a trade, and it is not a substitute for the cause of a move. If the block is missing the briefing says so.

## 2. Read it like an analyst
- How big is the move in daily ranges (ATR)? Over two is unusual. Is it market-wide or one market?
- Is there a cause in the headlines or the calendar (CPI, jobs, FOMC, geopolitics)? Say if you cannot find one.
- Where is price against its 20-day range (breakdown, breakout, middle)? What are IG clients doing (extreme positioning is only a hypothesis, never a rule)?
- Open positions, the £15 floor, the cooldown after two losses, and the drawdown brake. Read the equity line.
- For a sudden drop, the honest answer is often "no trade". Fading and following a spike both lack a validated edge: if you cannot pick a side with a stated reason, record NO idea (never both directions: one always loses and the record then looks like a coin flip), and leave a `manage note` saying why. Only record an idea you would back. If an idea was recorded without a real view, tag it with `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge exclude --idea-id N --reason "..."` so it stays out of the results.

## 2b. Price-setup scan (fixed criteria, no news needed; added 2026-11-12, see RULE_CHANGELOG.md)
Go down the briefing's range-position column every check. Do not wait for a news story.
- **Breakout or breakdown** (playbook `trend`): range position above 100% means a long, below 0% a short, provided IG clients are not already 70% or more positioned the same way. The stop is about 1.3 ATR, the target twice the stop, horizon 5 days.
- **Fade** (playbook `other`): a move of more than 2 ATRs with no cause found after a search, traded against the move.
- Each trigger is an idea with an honest `--p-win` (about 0.36 at 2 to 1: momentum evidence is thin, never raise it to pass Kelly). Markets that do not fit the account are recorded as shadow ideas. Take at most one live per check, the cleanest one, if the guardrails pass. Say plainly when several triggers are the same bet (indices together, dollar pairs together). Do not change the criteria after seeing results.

## 3. Write the ideas (zero to three)
For every idea you would seriously consider, record it with `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge idea --market ... --direction ... --stop <points> --target <points> --p-win <0.05 to 0.95> --playbook <event_reaction|news_thesis|earnings|trend|sentiment|other> --confidence <LOW|MEDIUM|HIGH> --thesis "<at most 300 characters>"`.
- Aim for at least 2 to 1 reward to risk. Stops are in points from the entry. Use sensible stops (about 1 to 1.5 daily ranges), not stops sized to fit the account.
- `--p-win` must be honest. Typical is 0.35 to 0.45. Never above 0.5 without a strong, stated reason. Overconfidence is the thing the calibration report exists to catch.
- Record the ideas the user will not take live too. Do not cherry-pick afterwards.
- The tool prints the 1/8 Kelly stake. If Kelly says do not trade, the idea stays shadow-only. Since 2026-11-12 the live stake is the market's minimum stake until my probabilities are calibrated (Kelly is then a skip filter only).

## 4. The live candidate (at most one per check): place it yourself
The account exists to gather data (user, 2026-11-12), but a live trade needs a genuine, confident directional view: never a guess, never a coin flip, and never a win chance raised to pass Kelly. When you do have that view and every condition below holds, place it without holding back out of excess caution. With no view, record shadow ideas and do not trade. Never use `--ignore-kelly`.
The user's standing instruction (2026-11-11, for the £45 challenge account only): place orders as part of this skill without asking each time. So do not wait for a yes. The guardrails in code still apply and are not negotiable.

Place it only if ALL of these hold:
- Kelly is positive (never use `--ignore-kelly`), the dry run says the order is allowed, and the profile in its first line is `challenge`.
- The stake fits (the dry run shows it), fewer than two positions are open, none is already open in that market, and the daily limit of two new trades is not reached.
- The market is tradeable and the spread on the dry run is no more than about three times its normal level from the briefing.
- There is a stated thesis, an invalidation (the stop), and an honest `--p-win`. You recorded the idea first with `idea` (record it live with `--live` if you place it).

How: run the dry run `python3 -m ig_swing.place --market ... --direction ... --stop ... --target ... --p-win ... --playbook ... --thesis "..." --idea-id N`, check it passes, then place it straight away with `IG_LIVE_ORDERS=yes python3 -m ig_swing.place ...` (same arguments) `--confirm <token>`. Then tell the user at once, in plain words: what, why, stake, stop level IG attached, target, risk in pounds and percent, the deal id, and that it is a gamble with no shown edge.

**Stop and ask the user instead of placing** if: any IG error or rate-limit message appears; the dry run is refused or the profile is not `challenge`; anything about the market or the order looks unusual or you are unsure of something material; the user's latest message told you to hold off. Show them the dry run and ask.

Opening is one half of this authorisation; managing open positions (step 0) is the other. IG holds each stop server-side.

## 5. Report
Say what you saw, what you recorded, what (if anything) is proposed live, and what you do not know. Mention `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge report` for the scored shadow book and the probability check. Keep it short.

**Journal it too.** `manage limit` and `manage trailing` now require `--why`, and `manage stop` takes `--why`; use the same words as in the chat. At the end of every check run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage note --market "<market>" --saw "<what you saw>" --decision "<what you did, or no action>"` for each market you seriously looked at, including the ones you left alone. It is journal only and places nothing.

**Whenever you place, close, reverse or change anything (a new order, a close, a stop or take-profit move, a trailing stop), tell the user in the chat straight away, in plain text, no artifact and no tables: what you did (market, direction, stake, stop, target, or the old and new level), and why in two to four sentences: what you saw that formed the decision (the move, the news or event, the level, IG sentiment, the spread), how sure you are (your stated win chance) and what would prove you wrong. For a close or a stop move, say what changed since entry. If you looked at something and did nothing, one line on why not.**

## 6. The learning loop (keep the data clean, do not tune on noise)
- **Run the full briefing at least once on every day we work.** Each run archives every market's conditions (price, move, volatility, range position, IG client sentiment) in the journal and records that day's mechanical baseline idea per market (long above the 20-day average, else short; 1.5 ATR stop, 3 ATR target, 5 days). Every idea you record automatically saves the conditions at that moment (move in ATRs, range position, IG sentiment, events due, hour, weekday). This is the dataset any later analysis depends on.
- **Record every idea you seriously consider**, including ones you do not take live, and never edit one afterwards.
- **Run `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge review` about weekly, or when the user asks.** It compares my ideas with the dumb baseline on the same market and day, splits results by playbook, confidence, research depth and exit style, checks my stated probabilities against what happened, and shows closed live trades by exit reason. It says plainly when there are too few results.
- **Never change a rule on fewer than about 30 results per playbook, and change one thing at a time.** Substantive changes to staking, limits or the guardrails need the user's explicit go. Every rule change gets a dated line in `RULE_CHANGELOG.md` before it takes effect, so results can be split into before and after, and a change is judged only on ideas written after it. Do not re-score old ideas to fit a tweak, do not slice results by many features on small numbers, and say how many variants have been tried.
- Machine learning is a later question (about a year of data): any model must beat the baseline and my own judgement on data it has not seen, by a margin set in advance, and would only ever be a second vote.

- **Back up the journal at the end of every check and session:** `python3 -m ig_swing.backup export --commit --push`. It writes plain text files to the backup folder, pushed to the `journal-backup` branch (never main) (`$IG_BACKUP_DIR`, default `../IG-Swing-backup`), never to this repo's working tree. If it says there is no remote, tell the user once; the backup is then local only. Restore on a new laptop with `python3 -m ig_swing.backup restore`.

## Never
- Place an order outside the conditions in step 4, with `--ignore-kelly`, when the profile is not `challenge`, or on any account other than the challenge account.
- Change the risk caps, the floor, the cooldown, the Kelly fraction or any limit, or use `--ignore-kelly`, unless the user explicitly asks.
- Remove or widen a stop, add to a position, or average down.
- Retry IG calls in a loop after a rate-limit error, or print credentials.
- Claim an edge. Do not push to the remote without the user's go.
