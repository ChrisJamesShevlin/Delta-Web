# Market check (the £45 challenge)

Read the section "The £45 play-money challenge" in CLAUDE.md first. Those rules decide everything below. Nothing here is a proven edge: say so, plainly, every time.

## The two standing goals (user instruction, 2026-11-19). Every check serves these first.
1. **Keep constantly abreast of the news, for every open trade and for possible trades.** Every check runs `python3 -m ig_swing.newswatch` (free feeds, no IG allowance): each open trade's headlines since entry, marked NEW since its last review, and everything in the last 3 hours that names any market. Plus `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge results` (releases in the last 24 hours, actual against forecast, no IG calls) and one web search per open trade on the risk named at entry. Read the story behind any headline that could move a held market. A possible trade found in the news is weighed in the same check (there is no positions-only check any more).
2. **Never let a good gain turn into nothing.** The user watched about £5 of open profit shrink to about £2.50 while I kept refusing to move stops into profit. That refusal is over. At EVERY check, before anything else:
   - **The stop logic (from the pre-registered stop-rule test of 2026-11-19, see CLAUDE.md), at every check:**
     1. Run `IG_LIVE_ORDERS=yes python3 -m ig_swing.manage trail --execute` at every check: stop to breakeven at +0.5R; from +0.75R the stop locks at least +0.25R and sits 0.75R behind the best price. It only ever tightens.
     2. **From +0.5R, put IG's trailing stop on and leave it on, day and night:** `IG_LIVE_ORDERS=yes python3 -m ig_swing.manage trailing --deal-id ... --why "..." --execute`. It trails at the gap at that moment, never looser than step 1, and works between checks. `manage list` flags PUT THE TRAILING STOP ON until it is done. Never take it off.
     3. On a position IG is already trailing, `manage trail` leaves it alone; compare its stop with step 1 by eye and tighten by hand with `manage stop` if step 1 would be tighter.
     Why: in the test, breakeven-only rules (what we ran until 19 Nov) left 24 to 31% of trades that reached +0.75R ending at zero or worse; this logic cut that to 5% at a cost of about 0.01R a trade, inside the noise. The stop rule does not change the money much either way; the entries decide that.
   - Act on any `GIVING BACK PROFIT` flag in `manage list` (a trade that reached +0.5R or more at a check and has handed back half): tighten so at least half the best is kept, and if that stop would sit at or through the price, take the profit with `manage close --reason-type profit_taking`. Do not wait to be asked.
   - Before the last check of the day, before the weekend, and before a high-impact release that can hit a held market: every position in profit gets its stop into profit (at least +0.25R if it has been +0.75R) and, from +0.5R, IG's trailing stop if it is not on yet.
   - Protecting profit is a stated reason for a stop move or a close. "Not tuning on noise" is never a reason to leave a winner unprotected.

## One mode: every check is a full market check (user decision, 2026-11-19)
The separate positions-only "health check" is gone: on 19 Nov it spotted natural gas above its 20-day high but could not trade it, and by the market check half an hour later the chance had gone. "Market check", "health check", "check" and "full check" all mean this, in this order, every time:
1. **Positions first** (sections 0 and 0a): `manage reconcile --execute`; `IG_LIVE_ORDERS=yes manage trail --execute`; `manage trailing ... --execute` on anything at +0.5R or more that IG is not trailing; `manage book`; `manage list`; act on every ATTENTION line.
2. **News** (goal 1): `python3 -m ig_swing.newswatch`, `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge results`, and a web search on each open trade's named risk.
3. **The written review of every open position** (`manage review`, section 0a), then do what each verdict says.
4. **The full briefing** (`challenge brief`, section 1) and `quick` on US 500 and US Tech 100.
5. **The near-the-edge list** (section 2b): every market beyond its 20-day range AND every market within 0.25 ATR of its 20-day high or low. Name each one in the reply; the ones near the edge are re-checked at the next check.
6. **Research pass** (section 1b): at least three searches, PLUS one fixed search beyond the list every check (user go, 2026-11-19): a WebSearch for today's biggest movers (US and UK stocks, commodities, currencies, anything with a sharp move or big news). For each one that is not on the briefing list and looks like a trigger, run `challenge find` and `challenge quick` (any IG daily bet works); name the ones checked in the reply, with "none found" when there are none. `challenge movers` does NOT do this: it only ranks the same list against stored bars. If the search returns nothing dated today, say so.
7. **Ideas** (section 3) for every trigger and anything seriously considered; every idea that passes section 4 is placed live, with `--exit-line` (no per-check limit, user 2026-11-19).
8. **Journal notes** for every market looked at, the **report page** (`report_check`, section 5), then the **backup**.
9. **The reply** in the order of section 5, with the IG allowance left and the points this check used.
**The last check of the day** is any check after 20:00 UTC (or when the user says it is the last one): it also applies the daily-close exit lines (a close beyond the exit line closes the trade), and moves every trade in profit to a stop in profit with IG's trailing stop on, before the night. On a Friday it is the weekend check too.
**News the user pastes in** (a wire story, a notification): journal it with `manage note`, then at once say what it means for each open position and whether it is a possible trade; if it touches a held market, run that position's review (section 0a) straight away rather than waiting for the next check.

## 0a. The position review: re-decide every open trade, every check (user instruction, 2026-11-19)
Why it exists: on 19 Nov the crude short sat back above its own exit line (the old 20-day low, about 8316) while I compared it with the current 20-day range, which had reset after a new low, and wrote a different level (8374) in my notes. An Iran-strike headline was noted but never weighed. The user had to push three times before I said the trade should go. The user's point: one stored number is not enough either, because news changes the outlook as time goes on. So every check re-decides every open trade from scratch, and writes the answer down.

For each open position, at EVERY check:
1. **Exit line.** `manage list` prints the price against the exit line stored for that deal (`exit_line` event), as CROSSED, NEAR (within 0.25 daily ranges) or clear. Never compare with the current 20-day range instead: it resets. Every new order must give `--exit-line` to `ig_swing.place` (it is refused if the line is not between the entry and the stop); for a breakout it is the old edge of the range. Set or correct one with `manage exitline --deal-id ... --level ... --why ...`, and never move it to make a trade look better.
2. **News since entry.** `newswatch` prints the headlines on that market since the trade opened (not just the last 12 hours), marked NEW since the last review; read the ones that matter. Then a web search on the specific risk named at entry (for example Iran and Hormuz for crude, the Fed and the dollar for dollar trades). Data actuals against forecast for anything that has come out.
3. **The reason for the trade.** intact, weakened or broken: is the catalyst still working, played out, or reversed?
4. **What is due before the next check**: data, speeches, auctions, the weekend, and whether this trade is exposed to each.
5. **"Would I open this trade now, at this price, knowing what I know?"** yes, smaller or no.

Record it with `python3 -m ig_swing.manage review --deal-id ... --news "..." --thesis intact|weakened|broken --events "..." --would-open yes|smaller|no --verdict hold|tighten|close --why "..." --source ...` (journal only). The command refuses inconsistent answers: a "no" or a broken reason must be closed; "smaller" or "weakened" must be tightened or closed; a hold through a crossed exit line needs `--override` with the reason (for example waiting for the stated daily close); a hold with no exit line is refused. Then do what the verdict says, at once, under the standing authorisation.

**Raise it first, unprompted.** `manage list` ends with an ATTENTION block (exit line crossed or near, no exit line, not reviewed, review older than 3 hours, a close verdict not acted on). Anything there goes at the TOP of the chat reply with my recommendation, before anything else. The user must never have to push for it. The report page shows the reviews and flags. The reviews are journalled so we can later check whether closing on "I would not open it now" helped or hurt.

## 0. Review open positions FIRST, before looking for anything new
First run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage reconcile --execute` (it records in the journal any trade IG closed on its own, by stop or take-profit, since the last session; it changes the journal only and places nothing; if it reports a close it could not match, tell the user), then the profit ratchet and the news watch from "The two standing goals" above. Then run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage book` (the open book grouped by driver: stacked bets, combined stop risk, what happens if they all turn together; information only, state it in the report) and `python3 -m ig_swing.manage list` at the start of every check (it also logs a position check, so the best and worst excursion can be estimated later). Managing an open trade is risk management and comes before new ideas. For each position look at its P&L in R, its stop and take-profit, the thesis and invalidation recorded at entry, and what has changed since. Then act, unprompted, under the user's standing authorisation (2026-11-11). The user's point: you trade without emotion, so you are better placed to decide, and **cutting losers short is where most traders fail**.

You may, without asking:
- **Close a position in loss or in profit** with `python3 -m ig_swing.manage close --deal-id ... --reason-type <thesis_invalidated|new_information|risk_event|time|reversal|profit_taking|other> --reason "<why>" --execute` (with `IG_LIVE_ORDERS=yes`). **Cut a loser early, before its stop, when its thesis is broken**: the invalidation you recorded has happened (a level broken, the catalyst reversed, the data went the other way) or the facts have changed. Never hold a dead thesis because the stop is not hit yet, and never hope.
- **Tighten a stop** (`manage stop ... --level|--breakeven|--distance`) or run the mechanical rule every check: `manage trail --execute` (user instruction 2026-11-19: stop to breakeven at +0.5R; from +0.75R it locks at least +0.25R and trails 0.75R behind the price, ratcheting every check). **Set or move the take-profit** (`manage limit`).
- **Reverse**: close with `--reason-type reversal`, then record a NEW idea in the opposite direction with its own honest probability, stop and target, and place it with `ig_swing.place`. A reversal is a fresh assessment, never "to win it back". The cooldown after two losing trades still applies (there is no daily cap on new trades since 2026-11-12), and a cut loser counts as a loss: if they block the new entry, say so and do not look for a way round.
- **Profit protection (user instruction and stop-rule test, 2026-11-19).** Follow the stop logic in "The two standing goals" at every check: `manage trail --execute`, IG's trailing stop on from +0.5R and left on (`manage trailing --deal-id ... --why ... --execute` keeps the current stop and asks IG to trail at the current gap, so it can never widen), and the GIVING BACK PROFIT rule. It worked on its first live use (silver, 19 Nov); check it with `manage list` ("IG trailing N points"). `manage trail` leaves positions IG is trailing alone, so on a trailed position tighten by hand with `manage stop` if the trail is too loose. If IG refuses the trailing call, the command prints manual steps for the user. The shadow book still scores fixed against trailing exits, so we can see what the protection costs.
- **Let winners run, with the profit locked.** The take-profit set at entry is only a first target. When a position is near it, the thesis is intact and the move is still going, move the take-profit further out with `manage limit`, but only with the stop already locking profit (never just breakeven). Take the profit instead (`--reason-type profit_taking`) when the move is stalling, the thesis has been played out, or a risk event is due. Record why each time, so `manage history` can show whether letting winners run paid.
- Before a high-impact event (CPI, jobs, FOMC, a central bank) in a market where you hold a position that is not an event trade: a trade in profit gets its stop into profit, a trade at or below breakeven gets tightened or closed. The same before the weekend.
- At the end of an idea's time horizon with neither stop nor target hit, reassess and close if the thesis is not working (`--reason-type time`).

Rules for early exits: tie each one to a stated reason (protecting profit counts), not to discomfort or the clock. They are discretionary and can hurt (cutting a trade that would have worked), so run `python3 -m ig_swing.manage history` now and then: it compares early exits with stop and target hits, which is how we learn whether cutting early helps. Run each changing command first without `--execute` if you are unsure; the dry run shows exactly what would change.

Never: widen a stop, remove a stop, add to a position, average down, or touch an account other than the challenge account. A stop can only tighten (the client refuses otherwise). Tell the user at once after any action, in plain words: what, why, the result. Between sessions only IG's server-side stop and take-profit protect a position, so be conservative about leaving a position unprotected from a risk event.

A later check on the same day is never a reason to skip the briefing.

What the data does and does not cover, so you do not overclaim: the calendar now has two layers (2026-11-17): the US releases from BLS and FRED plus the Fed and central bank decision days (ECB, BoE, BoJ, RBA, BoC, RBNZ, SNB), and a free world calendar (euro area, UK, Japan, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Switzerland, China and the US) with release times, an impact rating, the consensus forecast, the previous value and, once released, the ACTUAL figure. ## 1b. Research pass (for any idea you might take live, and for any sudden move you cannot explain)
**Every full market check runs at least three searches, even with no live candidate** (user decision, 2026-11-17): (1) the main driver of the dollar and yields today (Fed speakers, rate pricing); (2) the cause of any market that moved more than 1.5 ATR, held or not; (3) what is expected from the next high-impact release and what is priced in, especially if a position is held through it. Note what you found in the journal notes. Research is for understanding and avoiding bad trades, not an edge: never raise `--p-win` because a story sounds convincing.
Use WebSearch and WebFetch for about three to six lookups, not an open-ended trawl. Aim to find:
- the **cause and timing** of the move, from a primary or wire source (a central bank statement, the BLS or other official release, Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, the company filing) rather than a blog;
- for a data release, the **forecast against the actual number**: the surprise is what moves price, and the free calendar has no forecasts;
- **how much is already priced in**: when the news broke and how far price has moved since, because a move that has already happened is not a trade;
- the other side: the best argument against the idea, and what would prove it wrong.
- IG's own public analyst page, `https://www.ig.com/uk/news-and-trade-ideas` (WebFetch, no login): read the newest items and any article about the market you are weighing (earnings previews, the levels IG's analysts watch). It is commentary published a few times a day, not a live wire, and it is not the platform's in-app news or push alerts, which sit behind the user's login and which the API does not give us. If the user pastes a headline from a notification, treat it as the freshest source and say whether it gives a direction you would back. Count it as a web source in `--research web --source <url>`.
Record how deep you went with `--research none|headlines|web` and the sources you relied on with `--source` (repeatable). More reading is not an edge: markets absorb news in seconds and you are reading it late, so do not raise `--p-win` because a story sounds convincing. A persuasive narrative found after the move is a reason for suspicion. The report compares results by research depth, which is how we find out whether reading more helps. Flag secondary or promotional sources as such.

## 1c. Technical read (added 2026-11-12)
The briefing prints a **Technical read** block for every market with daily bars, and `quick` prints one for the market you name: trend against the 20-day average and its five-day slope, RSI(14), distance in ATRs to the 20-day high and low (a minus sign means beyond it), and the prior day's and last five days' highs and lows. It is archived with every snapshot and saved with every idea. Use it to confirm or contradict a driver and to place stops and targets at real levels, not as a signal on its own (the technical rules alone showed no edge). A news idea that fights the trend, or a breakout into an extreme RSI, gets a lower honest `--p-win` or no trade. Since 2026-11-12 it also carries a **longer view from Yahoo** (free, no IG allowance used): distance from the 50 and 200 day averages and whether they are aligned, and the US shares get a full read too. Yahoo is a different instrument from IG's daily bet (cash index, front future, spot currency), so its levels are rescaled and approximate; IG's own bars still set ATR and stops. If Yahoo fails the briefing says so and carries on.

## 1e. News sources and the calendar (widened 2026-11-12)
Headlines now come from about 40 free feeds fetched together (CNBC, MarketWatch, WSJ, NYT, BBC and Sky business, FT, Yahoo Finance UK and US, Reuters and Bloomberg headlines through Google News, FXStreet, InvestingLive, OilPrice, SCMP and more) and are matched to each market; the full day's pool is searched, not a top 40. They are titles only, aggregated, and the matching is crude (for example 'ECB filings' is not the central bank), so read the story behind anything that matters. Reddit is deliberately not used: it rate-limits scripts and is chatter, not news. The calendar now also has the decision days of the ECB, Bank of England, Bank of Japan, RBA, RBNZ, Bank of Canada and SNB, dates only (no times), with a currency in each label; the SNB, RBNZ and ECB dates were partly read from secondary sources, so re-check any date before relying on it for a trade. There are still no forecasts or consensus numbers.

**Levels (added 2026-11-12).** The technical read also shows daily pivot points (S2, S1, P, R1, R2 from the prior day), 5-day pivots, and swing support and resistance: the two nearest levels each side of price from the longer history, with the number of times price reacted there (a level that held four times counts for more than one that held once). Use them to place the trade, not to predict direction: put the stop just beyond the nearest level that would invalidate the idea, and the target at or just before the next level, so the reward to risk is real, not an ATR multiple. If the nearest level is closer than about one stop away in the direction of the target, say so: the reward to risk is worse than it looks. Levels from Yahoo are approximate (rescaled). Trend lines and extra oscillators are deliberately not used.

## 1d. Macro backdrop (added 2026-11-12)
The briefing prints a **Macro backdrop** block: the US 10-year yield, the dollar index and the VIX, with the move today and over five sessions (Yahoo, same day, no key). It is archived as a `macro_backdrop` event every briefing. Use it as context: a rising dollar and yield usually press on gold and on currencies against the dollar, and a jumping VIX says risk appetite is falling. No rule turns it into a trade, and it is not a substitute for the cause of a move. If the block is missing the briefing says so.

## 2. Read it like an analyst
- How big is the move in daily ranges (ATR)? Over two is unusual. Is it market-wide or one market?
- Is there a cause in the headlines or the calendar (CPI, jobs, FOMC, geopolitics)? Say if you cannot find one.
- Where is price against its 20-day range (breakdown, breakout, middle)? What are IG clients doing (extreme positioning is only a hypothesis, never a rule)?
- Open positions, the £15 floor, the cooldown after two losses, and the drawdown brake. Read the equity line.
- For a sudden drop, the honest answer is often "no trade". Fading and following a spike both lack a validated edge: if you cannot pick a side with a stated reason, record NO idea (never both directions: one always loses and the record then looks like a coin flip), and leave a `manage note` saying why. Only record an idea you would back. If an idea was recorded without a real view, tag it with `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge exclude --idea-id N --reason "..."` so it stays out of the results.

## 2b. Price-setup scan (fixed criteria, no news needed; added 2026-11-12, see RULE_CHANGELOG.md)
Go down the briefing's range-position column every check. Do not wait for a news story. **Near the edge (added 2026-11-19):** also list every market within 0.25 ATR of its 20-day high or low (the technical read's "+0.1 ATR below the 20-day high"), say in the reply what would trigger it (a close beyond the level, with the number), and look at it again at the next check. Touching the level and falling back is not a trigger: the crude short of 18 Nov was exactly that kind of failed break.
- **Breakout or breakdown** (playbook `trend`): range position above 100% means a long, below 0% a short, provided IG clients are not already 70% or more positioned the same way. The stop is about 1.3 ATR, the target twice the stop, horizon 5 days.
- **Fade** (playbook `other`): a move of more than 2 ATRs with no cause found after a search, traded against the move.
- Each trigger is an idea with an honest `--p-win` (about 0.36 at 2 to 1: momentum evidence is thin, never raise it to pass Kelly). Markets that do not fit the account are recorded as shadow ideas. Take at most one live per check, the cleanest one, if the guardrails pass. Say plainly when several triggers are the same bet (indices together, dollar pairs together). Do not change the criteria after seeing results.

## 3. Write the ideas (zero to three)
For every idea you would seriously consider, record it with `python3 -m ig_swing.challenge idea --market ... --direction ... --stop <points> --target <points> --p-win <0.05 to 0.95> --playbook <event_reaction|news_thesis|earnings|trend|sentiment|other> --confidence <LOW|MEDIUM|HIGH> --thesis "<at most 300 characters>"`.
- Aim for at least 2 to 1 reward to risk. Stops are in points from the entry. Use sensible stops (about 1 to 1.5 daily ranges), not stops sized to fit the account.
- `--p-win` must be honest. Typical is 0.35 to 0.45. Never above 0.5 without a strong, stated reason. Overconfidence is the thing the calibration report exists to catch.
- Record the ideas the user will not take live too. Do not cherry-pick afterwards.
